# Directory playbook

## How to use this catalog

These 13 destinations come from a real startup-listing campaign. Operational observations below were recorded in September–October 2026; **recheck the current site, free tier, terms, limits, badge requirements, and publication route on every run**. This is not evidence that a new startup is eligible or that all historical submissions succeeded. Do not copy the source startup's identity, account IDs, pricing, or badges into a new campaign.

Start from the public entry points below or an owner-provided authenticated tab. Follow observed links for product-specific dashboards and slugs. If a route redirects or a marketplace changes operators, inspect the live destination rather than inventing URLs or creating a duplicate product.

## Platform-specific lessons

### TinyLaunch and TinyShelf

Capture the current embed rather than reconstructing it from memory. TinyLaunch previously supplied a `tinylaunch_badge_launching_soon.svg` image: retain the correct launch state and do not replace it with an award or featured claim. TinyShelf used a reciprocal badge. A production homepage check may be required before submission can finish; an undeployed local component does not satisfy it.

### Product Hunt

Restoring a badge requires the actual product/post identifier and appropriate badge variant. Search the repository and Git history, existing account, and public product page. Preserve a verified existing listing instead of inventing a new one. A historical launch badge is not proof of a current rank, award, vote count, or new launch.

### Trustpilot

The historical signup required phone, employee range, and annual revenue range. These cannot be inferred from a small repository or an individual founder. Ask only for missing required values, or have the owner enter them directly.

The owner of the source campaign requested this badge in the hero; that was a campaign-specific exception. Record the current owner's placement preference. Do not display invented stars, review counts, or a TrustScore. Use a permitted, truthful profile link or badge supported by the current account and brand requirements.

### SourceForge

Creating a product submission and registering an account are distinct steps. Check whether either already exists. The historical form combined Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, and agreement to receive communications in one required checkbox. Show a materially bundled consent to the owner if existing authorization does not cover it; do not hide the marketing opt-in.

Browser verification required owner participation in the source campaign. Ask for the specific verification action and verify the resulting state afterward. For account registration, let the owner enter/save the password directly, then continue the authorized flow. Do not describe proprietary software as open source to use a different submission path.

### G2

Search for the existing product before using the new-product form. The source campaign hit “Product name is already taken”; the correct next action was to use the existing product's admin area, not alter its name to evade the duplicate check.

Complete all useful free sections, including description, logo, screenshots, accurate pricing, and supported AI capabilities. Review AI-drafted pricing carefully: a monthly figure can actually be an annual-billing equivalent. AI-enabled declarations and verified designations have different requirements; do not claim a verification badge simply because the product uses AI.

Ignore irrelevant or paid completion pressure. First/5/20-review milestones need real eligible customer reviews. PPC ads, upgraded CTAs, custom banners, featured placements, and some widgets can be paid. Do not manufacture reviews, buy upgrades, or invite a backup admin just to finish the progress meter. If the owner elects to remain sole admin, preserve that choice.

### Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice

In the October 2026 source campaign, an existing my.G2 product exposed **Listings → Manage Listings** and separate marketplace cards. The official Capterra vendor flow also led to G2. Navigate the current UI and reuse the actual product; do not assume every future campaign shares this manager.

Historical required sections and constraints are debugging clues, not current specifications:

| Marketplace | Required content observed | Constraints observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capterra | Long description, short description, target market, AI capabilities, screenshots, video | 1,000 / 135 / 200 / 1,000 characters respectively; captions up to 255; up to 5 screenshots, under 5 MB; one supported video URL. |
| GetApp | Long description, short description, benefits, tagline, screenshots, video | Long description/benefits displayed 4,000 characters, tagline 60. **Short description displayed 300 but backend accepted at most 135.** A 127-character version persisted. Category-specific description had its own save form. |
| Software Advice | Long description, screenshots, video | Long description displayed 4,000 characters; up to 6 screenshots and one video were offered. |

The accepted video providers included YouTube, Vimeo, Vidyard, and Wistia. Verify current requirements; use the owner's actual product video. A missing video can block Publish even when descriptions and screenshots are saved.

If a section appears to reset, inspect validation and which form's Save control was used. Do not repeatedly overwrite a long description when the real error is the short-description limit. Save default and category-specific fields separately where applicable, then revisit each to confirm persistence.

All three source-campaign dashboards eventually said Published, while the generated public URLs still returned 404. Preserve that exact distinction in the ledger. Do not resubmit duplicate products to solve propagation/moderation delays, and do not claim the public listings are live based on a progress card alone.

### Fazier

The historical free route required a homepage badge and three public comments. Recheck whether those requirements still apply. Listing approval does not automatically authorize public comments: prepare useful, specific drafts based on actually reading the recipient products and request approval for those exact messages. Do not fabricate product use, write generic praise in bulk, solicit reciprocal votes, or impersonate a customer.

Prepare logo, actual product gallery, description, features, use cases, categories, and any final maker comment or competitor fields. A working manual file picker can coexist with an automation failure. Use supported upload tools or give the owner prepared files and one exact manual step. After the gallery succeeds, inspect the remaining final form; completing the gallery is not submission proof.

### Startup Fame and Twelve Tools

Startup Fame previously supplied a product-specific `/s/<slug>` destination and a configurable badge endpoint. Twelve Tools supplied a white badge variant. Always use the actual current embed and test contrast against the startup's footer background. A supplied “Featured on” image alone is not evidence of acceptance; verify the permitted use and listing state before displaying its claim.

### Peerlist

Keep profile, project, Scroll post, and Launchpad launch separate. Reuse an existing public project or post instead of duplicating it. Fill project description, actual technology stack, website, logo, and screenshots; derive stack labels from the codebase rather than copying generic defaults.

The source owner declined verification after encountering a paid step. Keep completed free project/Scroll work and record Launchpad as blocked/declined if that is the current situation. Do not buy verification or assume an unverified project is unusable. Existing public project links were observed with `nofollow ugc`; inspect the actual new link before reporting its attributes.

## Discover more relevant free routes

Use search to find category, regional, developer, or founder directories appropriate to the product. Verify the submit route, eligibility, audience, fee, badge, moderation/schedule, and sample public pages using primary sources. Aggregator lists are leads, not proof of free availability or dofollow links. Search the product's domain on each candidate first to avoid duplicates.

An earlier related campaign also encountered SubmitForBacklinks, Dodo Payments Index, Uneed, CodeHype, Aura++, Nick Launches, ctrlalt, BetterLaunch, and What Launched Today. These are optional discovery leads, not verified-current free defaults. Look up their official routes and record current conditions before adding them to the approved inventory. Reject an unrelated service or a paid-only route with a reason rather than forcing it into the campaign.

## Backlink and outcome claims

Record what can be observed: the public page, the actual link target and `rel`, and later referrals or conversions if available. DR is Ahrefs' relative backlink-profile metric; a directory's high DR alone does not establish the value of a particular listing. [Ahrefs explains DR and the additional factors in evaluating links](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/1409408-what-is-domain-rating-dr).

Required reciprocal badges are a campaign condition the owner may accept, not a guaranteed SEO improvement. Google's policy explicitly discusses excessive link exchanges and low-quality directory links. Include that distinction briefly in a DR-focused review pack without repeatedly blocking authorized, truthful listings. [Google's link-spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam).

These two explanatory sources were checked October 17, 2026. Recheck them when making current SEO recommendations. Never promise ranking credit, indexing, or a numeric DR gain from badge deployment.
