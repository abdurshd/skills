# Surface modes and direction

Choose the mode from the visitor’s job on the target surface. Do not apply marketing-site rules to a dashboard or product-UI restraint to a portfolio.

## Persuade

Use for landing pages, marketing sites, pricing, campaigns, and conversion surfaces.

The visitor must understand what this is, why it matters, why to believe it, and what to do next within seconds.

Prioritize:

- an intelligible offer and visible primary action;
- a first viewport that demonstrates the product mechanism or strongest proof;
- product-specific imagery, artifacts, examples, and copy;
- deliberate pacing across dense and quiet sections;
- a visual point of view strong enough to be remembered.

Permit:

- committed or drenched color;
- expressive display typography;
- asymmetric or image-led composition;
- one authored motion sequence.

Reject:

- generic hero plus dashboard mockup plus feature cards;
- benefits without evidence;
- every section using the same card or centered-heading grammar;
- safe restraint that erases the brand;
- decorative effects substituting for missing imagery or content.

The slop test is distinctiveness: could a competitor reuse the page after changing the logo and nouns?

## Operate

Use for apps, dashboards, editors, admin, settings, forms, tables, and authenticated product UI.

The user must complete a task with confidence. Familiarity is often a feature; “different” is not automatically better.

Prioritize:

- obvious task and state;
- consistent components and terminology;
- stable density and scanability;
- conventional affordances where convention reduces thought;
- complete loading, empty, error, disabled, success, and permission states;
- fast state and continuity motion, usually 150–300 ms.

Permit:

- one workhorse type family or a system stack;
- standard navigation, tabs, breadcrumbs, tables, and command palettes;
- dense information when the task requires it;
- restrained color reserved for action, selection, focus, and status.

Reject:

- display faces in labels or data;
- decorated buttons and reinvented controls;
- full-page entrance choreography;
- inactive controls competing with active state;
- modals as the first solution to complex tasks;
- brand expression that obscures familiar operation.

The slop test is earned familiarity: would a category-fluent user trust the interface immediately, or pause at subtly wrong controls and states?

## Read

Use for documentation, articles, guides, changelogs, policies, and help.

The reader must understand, navigate, and retain information.

Prioritize:

- clear document outline and wayfinding;
- a comfortable 45–75ch prose measure;
- meaningful heading hierarchy and paragraph rhythm;
- citations, examples, diagrams, and code that support comprehension;
- stable anchors, search, and table-of-contents behavior;
- responsive reflow and 200% zoom.

Permit:

- quiet layouts and workhorse type;
- restrained navigation and utility controls;
- cards only for genuinely independent resources or actions.

Reject:

- cardifying every paragraph or section;
- decorative editorial affectations unrelated to the material;
- repeated intros, summaries, and helper copy saying the same thing;
- motion that interrupts reading;
- narrow gray text chosen to “feel elegant.”

The slop test is comprehension: does the design clarify the material, or merely style it?

## Experience

Use for portfolios, galleries, showcases, interactive stories, and bodies of work.

The artifact must lead from the first viewport; the interface should frame exploration rather than compete with it.

Prioritize:

- the work itself at meaningful scale;
- a memorable exploration model;
- careful sequencing and transitions;
- concise context that improves interpretation;
- strong asset direction and loading behavior.

Permit:

- expressive composition and technically ambitious interactions;
- section-specific art direction within one voice;
- minimal navigation when orientation remains clear.

Reject:

- a conventional agency landing page placed in front of the work;
- thumbnail card grids as the only exploration model;
- generic “creative” effects, cursors, marquees, and hover transforms;
- interfaces louder than the artifacts.

The slop test is authorship: does the experience reveal how this body of work should be seen?

## Refinement versus redesign

### Refinement

Preserve the incumbent identity, behavior, content, and system. Fix hierarchy, consistency, states, copy, responsive behavior, and implementation quality at the narrowest correct level.

Do not smuggle a new visual identity into “polish.” If the concept itself is the problem, report that and request redesign authority.

### Redesign

Preserve product truth, content meaning, function, constraints, and explicit brand commitments. Treat the old appearance as evidence and anti-reference, not as authority.

Do not split the difference between the discarded and replacement worlds. Choose one coherent replacement and carry it through navigation, controls, content, states, imagery, and motion.

## Product-grounded direction method

Use productive friction before code:

1. Name the product’s unique mechanism.
2. Describe the audience’s real scene: who, where, under what light, using what other tools or artifacts.
3. State what the first viewport must prove.
4. Name the category default and its predictable anti-default.
5. Derive references from the audience’s cultural or professional world: publications, notation systems, physical objects, tools, interfaces, places, rituals, or visual traditions.
6. Ensure the references span at least three material families; seven near-identical editorial references are one idea.
7. Turn references into complete systems, not mood-board fragments: palette, typography, topology, components, states, imagery, motion, and responsive rules.
8. Evaluate each on audience identification and product clarity.
9. Commit to one direction. Novelty that obscures the task loses.

Avoid prompts made only of adjectives such as “modern, premium, clean, bold.” They leave every material decision to the model’s statistical default.
